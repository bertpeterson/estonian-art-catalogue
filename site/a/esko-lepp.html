Esko Lepp

1906–1977 · Estonian printmaker (1906-1977)
Allee galerii; E-Kunstisalong; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Haus Galerii; Hiiumaa Muuseumid; Pärnu Muuseum

trained at Estonian Academy of Arts

Esko Lepp was born the son of a blacksmith. He studied graphic art and applied graphics at the State School of Applied Art in Tallinn from 1935-1940 (supervisor G. Reindorff). He was in the service of the Estonian National Museum (ERM). He furthered his education from 1940-1941 at the Konrad Mägi State Higher Art School in Tartu. In 1941 he joined a destruction battalion. During the Second World War he worked in a kolkhoz and a factory in Russia, joined the Red Army in 1942, was called up in 1943 to the Yaroslavl collective of Estonian artists, and also worked as a front-line draughtsman. From 1943-1948 he studied graphic art at the Surikov Art Institute in Moscow, graduating as a student of P. Pavlinov in the field of woodcut. From 1948-1951 Lepp was a lecturer at the Tallinn State Art Institute (ERKI), after which he became a freelance artist. He created works on war and industrial themes, series of woodcuts, illustrations, watercolours, and ex libris. (machine translation of the holder's Estonian text, unedited)
