Edgar Viies

1931–2006 · Estonian sculptor (1920-2006)
Allee galerii; E-Kunstisalong; Eesti Kunsti Oksjonid; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Haus Galerii; Tartu Kunstimuuseum

trained at Estonian Academy of Arts · trained at Imperial Academy of Arts

Edgar Viies (1931–2006) is one of the most important Estonian sculptors of the 20th century. His innovative formal searches and modern choice of materials (aluminium, clinker, welded iron, sheet metal and concrete) marked the beginning of the modern era in Estonian sculpture in the 1960s. With his work, a stylistically pure abstract formal culture appeared in Estonian sculpture for the first time in the post-war years. He already appeared with modernly generalised portrait work in exhibition halls in the late 1950s, attracting attention with his unusually pure silhouette effect and having an inspiring influence. Quite a few of his works from the 1960s–1980s have become classics of Estonian art. Besides the aesthetic problematics, which were important to Viies especially in the first half of his oeuvre, the experiential quality of his works and their connection to universal human spiritual and emotional life should also be valued. In the early 1960s he brought creative experiment into a relatively conservative and rather dull sculpture scene, directing sculptors' attention to the relations of space, form and material. It can be said that it was precisely Edgar Viies who gave impetus to decorative sculpture, which at the time existed in a state of suspended animation. His numerous free works belong to the golden fund of Estonian modernism. Viies's public sculptures in Tallinn, Tartu and Viljandi remain captivating to this day. The best known of these are the monuments to Johann Köler (1976), Jaan Koort (1983) and Friedebert Tuglas (1986), the "Mermaid" (1981) adorning Viru Square, and the soaring "Pegasus" (1963) at the café on Harju Street in Tallinn, which was familiar to several generations as the first modernist interior sculpture in Estonia. Edgar Viies studied in 1951–1952 at the State Art Institute of the ESSR. (machine translation of the holder's Estonian text, unedited)
