Osvald Timmas

1913–2005 · Estonian artist (1919-2005)

trained at University of Tartu

Osvald Timmas was born 17.09.1913 in Saaremaa, Kärla parish, Pai village, died 27.10.2005 in Toronto, Canada. He studied in the humanities branch of Saaremaa Joint Gymnasium and at the Tondi Military School in Tallinn, which he graduated from in 1934. He then studied at the school of the Estonian Consumer Cooperatives' Central Union (ETK) Cooperative Union and in 1937–1944 at the Faculty of Economics of the University of Tartu. He was a member of the fraternity Rotalia. In art, he took private lessons in 1939–1942 from Nikolai Kummits and in 1942–1944 from Günther Reindorff. In 1944 he fled together with his wife Erika to Sweden, where he worked in the life insurance field. In 1947–1949 he studied on illustration courses in Stockholm. In 1951 he moved to Toronto, Canada, where he also worked in life insurance. In 1956 he received Canadian citizenship. In the 1960s Timmas attracted attention with watercolours depicting geological layers of the earth's surface and the underwater world, as well as views seen through a microscope. In 1972 he devoted himself entirely to art. In 1961–1968 Timmas was president of the Colour and Form Society and in 1971–1975 vice-president of the Ontario Society of Artists. Timmas was also a founding member of the Estonian Arts Centre in Ontario in 1974. In 1973 O. Timmas was elected a member of the Royal Canadian Academy of Arts. In 1977 he received a decoration from the British Queen. In 2005 O. Timmas was awarded the Order of the White Star, 5th Class, of the Republic of Estonia. In his will he established the Erika and Osvald Timmas Memorial Fund, with the aim of preserving and developing Estonian culture and art. This fund has, for example, supported the building of Kumu, the activities of the Tartu Art Museum, the exhibition of Estonian émigré art in 2010–2011 in Tallinn, Tartu and elsewhere. Solo exhibitions in Estonia took place: in 1990 at the Adamson-Eric Museum, in 2003 at the National Library and at the Saaremaa Museum. (machine translation of the holder's Estonian text, unedited)
