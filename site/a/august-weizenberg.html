August Weizenberg

1837–1921 · Estonian sculptor (1837–1921)
E-Kunstisalong; Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Eesti Maaelumuuseumid SA, C. R. Jakobsoni Talumuuseum; Haus Galerii; Pärnu Muuseum

Came from a family of shoemakers. Worked in a joinery workshop in Erastvere. Was accepted to study in Frankfurt an der Oder, continued studies in Berlin, and on the recommendation of Fr. R. Kreutzwald, in St. Petersburg in the studio of sculptor A. F. von Bock, and 1865-1867 at the Imperial Academy of Arts in St. Petersburg. Worked in Tartu, Berlin, Munich, and 1873-1890 in Rome. Received the award of the Paris World Exhibition for the sculpture "Hamlet". From 1890 to 1914 he lived and worked in St. Petersburg, and thereafter until his death in Tallinn. From 1916 he served as overseer of the city's art treasures. The main part of his work consists of classicist allegorical figures and portraits. Throughout his career, but especially during his St. Petersburg years, he also created portraits of emperors and empresses, and in the 1890s participated in several competitions for monuments to rulers. The marble portrait bust of Alexander II that he created became especially popular, and the sculptor made several repetitions of it. He also portrayed other emperors and empresses who ruled during his lifetime - Alexander III and Maria Feodorovna, and Nicholas II and Alexandra Feodorovna.
