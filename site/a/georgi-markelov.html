Georgi Markelov

1929–2014 · Estonian sculptor (1929-2014)
Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum

trained at Estonian Academy of Arts

The sculptor Georgi Markelov (19.05.1929 – 15.01.2014) was born in Narva as the son of a construction entrepreneur and studied at Narva Primary School No. 6 from 1937 to 1943. Georgi Markelov's paternal home was also destroyed in wartime air raids. His father was deported to Siberia at the beginning of the Soviet occupation. After post-war studies at Väike-Maarja Secondary School, he entered the Estonian State Art Institute in 1950, graduating in 1956 in the field of sculpture. From that same year Markelov worked as a freelance artist in Tallinn; in 1959 he was accepted as a member of the Artists' Union, and in 1979 he was awarded the honorary title of Meritorious Artist by the government of the time. Georgi Markelov cultivated portrait sculpture in a realistic style, but he also created a series of 14 wood sculptures titled "Ei iialgi enam sõda" ("Never Again War"), several examples of which are held in the sculpture collection of the Art Museum of Estonia. In the 1970s–1980s he carved over-life-size portraits of V. I. Lenin from oak, most of which were acquired by state institutions. In turbulent times, some of these portrait examples also ended up in museum storage. The older generation of artists and art enthusiasts probably remember precisely these masterfully carved heads from the exhibitions of the Tallinn Art Hall's survey shows of the time. In 1975 the artist received the Kristjan Raud Prize for one such portrait. As a masterful carver, he also loved to create more lyrical small sculptures, flowers and birds, and wooden masks. In the summer of 2010 Markelov surprised both colleagues and the art audience with a solo exhibition at the gallery of the Monumental Art House on Raja Street. He presented an installation filling the entire gallery space, "Lilled emale" ("Flowers for Mother"), where the floor was covered with more than 6000 virtuosically carved flower blossoms of various kinds, made of mahogany, oak, ash, linden, etc. It was, as it were, a unique summation of the activity and standpoint of a sculptor — and of a person who had lived through various eras — regarding his place in today's world.
