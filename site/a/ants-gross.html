Ants Gross


Viljandi Muuseum

The Akstra technique - this technique was invented in Viljandi. The cultivation of Akstra art began in the second half of the 1970s under the guidance of A. Gross in the so-called "Akstra" studio. The Akstra technique was A. Gross's own invention. The main components of the technique were enamel paint (of a composition made by the artist himself, which he did not reveal to others) and diluted bitumen. Light enamel paint and dark bitumen were used on the principle of silkscreen printing, but instead of a silk frame a stencil was used. The parts that did not fall under the stencil had to be covered over, sprayed over with enamel paint using an atomiser, and then the enamel paint in turn had to be covered over with the bitumen mixture. The result was a pitch-black plate. Next one had to wait for a reaction, which resembled the developing of a photograph. The places that had been covered turned dark, while the light enamel paint left under the bitumen began, in turn, to react - pressing through the bitumen, and gradually, as a result of the reaction, the desired image was formed. The result of this kind of technique was a somewhat mysterious, watercolour-like painting with vague and sharp outlines. This technique could be executed with two colours.
