Johannes Greenberg

1887–1951 · Estonian painter (1887-1951)
Allee galerii; E-Kunstisalong; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Haus Galerii; Tartu Kunstimuuseum; Vaal galerii

trained at Academy of Fine Arts, Munich · trained at Anton Ažbe art school · trained at Atelier School of Ants Laikmaa

Greenberg was an Estonian painter whose work, following the artistic trends of the era, underwent a long stylistic development. The paintings created at the end of the 1920s are expressive, with a rough texture that adds expressiveness. In the 1930s Greenberg was influenced by art deco, in which the treatment of colour emphasises the plasticity of forms. In the middle of the same decade Greenberg's colouring lightened under the influence of impressionism, and he became fascinated by the world of theatre – the peculiar charm of actors and the charm of women quilted with poetic melancholy. During World War II, worry, grief and tragedy were added to his work, which the artist expressed using classical solutions. Greenberg received his art education at Ants Laikmaa's studio school in Tallinn, at the evening courses of the school of applied arts in Berlin, at A. Ažbe's art school in Munich, in H. Groeber's studio, and at the Munich Academy of Art. Later he worked as a lecturer at the Jaan Koort State School of Applied Art and at the Tallinn State Art Institute.
