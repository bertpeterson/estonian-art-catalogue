Kaljo Põllu

1934–2010 · Estonian artist (1934–2010)
Allee galerii; E-Kunstisalong; Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunsti Oksjonid; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Eesti Rahva Muuseum

trained at Estonian Academy of Arts

He began with free graphic art and abstract painting ("Kaarjas pingestatus" 1963). He was a founder and one of the leaders of the group "Visarid" (1967-72), creating during that time paintings influenced by pop art and op art ("Kurbus" 1968), and worked with kinetic art ("Ringlemine IV" 1966), assemblage ("Kandam" 1967), ready-made ("Vaikus" 1968) and collage ("Meelerahu" 1968). He was interested in psychedelic art. The themes of his later graphic art, made in mezzotint technique, are connected with Finno-Ugric mythology (series "Kodalased" 1973-75), express the aspirations of Estonians' national self-existence (series "Kalivägi" 1978-84), the relations between man and the universe (series "Taevas ja Maa" 1987-91), and the eternity of life (series "Kirgastumine" 1991-95). From 1993 he has made spatial objects ("Mõtteseisund" 1998) as well as paintings, the aim of which is to create the illusion of three-dimensionality ("Kaugustesse" 1998) and of impossible worlds ("Astmed" 1999). He has designed books and made ex libris. From 1978 he has, together with EKA students, studied the folk art of kindred peoples, stone labyrinths and Karelian rock carvings, and founded, from among the participants of these research trips, the group "Ydi" (1998). He has published writings on his creative and research work ("Kakskümmend soome-ugri uurimisreisi" 1999, "Hiiumaa rahvapärane ehituskunst" 2004).
