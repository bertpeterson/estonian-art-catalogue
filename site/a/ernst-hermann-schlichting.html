Ernst Hermann Schlichting

1812–1890 · Baltic German painter and lithographer (1812-1890)
Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Eesti Rahva Muuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Linnamuuseum

Born in Tallinn as the son of a merchant, he studied in Tartu under A. M. Hagen and, from 1834 to 1839, at the Dresden Academy of Art, and worked in Riga, Tallinn and St. Petersburg. In 1838 he unsuccessfully applied for the position of head of the drawing school at the University of Tartu. In 1845 he received the title of free artist from the St. Petersburg Academy of Arts. From 1850 to 1871 he was an art teacher at the Tallinn Cathedral School, and in 1853–54 ran a photography studio in Tallinn. In 1871 he moved to Dresden. In the 1850s he made a series of watercolours depicting Coastal Swedes, which were lithographed and published in 1854–1856 in C. F. W. Russwurm's scholarly book (Eibofolke...). He also painted landscapes, genre paintings and townscapes. (machine translation of the holder's Estonian text, unedited)
