Klara Zeidler

1870–1951 · Baltic German artist (1870-1951)
Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum

trained at Saint Petersburg Stieglitz State Academy of Art and Design

She was born as the daughter of Ferdinand Zeidler, who later ran a private school in Valga. She was educated in Viipuri and at the Valga private grammar school, and passed her final examinations in Tartu in 1887. From 1889–94 she studied at A. Stieglitz's School of Technical Drawing in St Petersburg, and as the school's scholarship holder studied porcelain painting in Paris and drawing at the Académie Colarossi. In 1908 she furthered her studies in metalwork in London, Stuttgart and Vienna. She worked as a teacher at art schools in Petrograd from 1897–1918. In 1918 she settled in Tallinn. From 1919–40 she was a teacher of metal, porcelain, watercolour and drawing at the State School of Applied Art. From 1924–35 she was head of the metal workshop and particularly developed filigree technique. From 1919–31 she was also a drawing teacher at the Tallinn German private grammar school for boys. She founded her own studio. In the 1920s she belonged to ARS, from 1928–35 to EKKKÜ, from 1938–39 to EAKKK, and took part in the founding of RaKü in 1932. In 1940 she moved to Finland, in 1941 to Germany, and in 1948 to Sweden. (machine translation of the holder's Estonian text, unedited)
