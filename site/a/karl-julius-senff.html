Karl Julius Senff

1804–1832
Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum

He was born as the eldest son of Karl August Senff, drawing teacher at the University of Tartu, and studied at the University of Tartu in 1823-27. He was supposed to become professor of architecture at the University of Tartu. On 12. XII 1827 K. J. Senff received a silver medal for answering philosophical questions; as a student he compiled a textbook on perspective for self-taught landscape painters (published 1825). In 1827 he received the degree of candidate of philosophy at the University of Tartu (his award-winning work "Systematische Darstellung der Hauptsätze der Geometrie im Raume" was printed at the university's expense). In 1829 he received a travel stipend for 3 years from the funds of the imperial Russian Ministry of National Education and travelled to Jena, where he received a doctorate in philosophy, then went to Prague and Vienna, where he studied architecture. In 1831 he stayed in Munich. He made 10 aquatint illustrations for his work "Praha Püha Vituse katedraal" (this was published in Berlin in 1831). Together with his uncle Karl Adolf Senff he travelled to Italy in 1831, where he lived in Rome, visited Naples and Pompeii. On the way back he fell ill with nervous fever and was brought unconscious to Milan, where he died. His works are found in the EKM, 72 in number (3 of them of doubtful authorship), and in the Tartu University library. Bibl: G. H. Vogel, manuscript of article, 2011.
