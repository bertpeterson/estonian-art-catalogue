Ants Eskola

1908–1989
Tartu Kunstimuuseum; Virumaa Muuseumid

Estonian actor, studied 1918–24 at the Tallinn Municipal Boys' Secondary School and 1924–25 at the State School of Arts and Crafts (in Günther Reindorff’s graphic art class). Studied singing under Armanda degli Abbati and Helmi Einer. Was admitted in 1925 to the Estonia study group, worked 1925–41 (also performed in operetta) and 1947–49 at Estonia, in 1947 at Rakvere Theatre, 1949–65 at the Drama Theatre and 1966–83 at the Youth Theatre; from 1941–46 was deported to Solikamsk. Performed in radio plays (Tolstoy’s The Living Corpse, 1956; Cronin’s The Citadel, 1956; Büchner’s Danton’s Death, 1963), television productions (Hauser’s White Blood, 1959; Gruszecki’s Big Bobby, 1962; Dürrenmatt’s The Meteor, 1969; Bunin’s On the Night Sea, 1977) and in more than 30 films (Children of the Sun, 1932, Theodor Luts’s studio; Pöördel, 1957, The New Devil of Hell's Bottom, 1964, Indrek, 1975, The Master of Kõrboja, 1979, all Tallinnfilm; Traces in the Snow, 1955, The Quiet Season, 1968, both Lenfilm; Ambassador of the Soviet Union, 1969, Mosfilm; Between Three Plagues, 1970, Eesti Telefilm). Was also known as a painter and, before World War II, as a mood singer. Memoir book Näitleja on ajastu lühikroonika (1986). Films have been made about him: Ants Eskola (1968, Eesti Telefilm) and Ants Eskola: olla või mitte olla (2008, director Kristjan Paul Virve). His prison letters have been published in the book Härra Ants. Ants Eskola kirjad Hugo Laurile Solikamski vangilaagrist (2005, compiled by Irja Vaher).
