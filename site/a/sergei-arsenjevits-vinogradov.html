Sergei Arsenjevitš Vinogradov

1869–1938
Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Viljandi Muuseum

From 1898–1913 he was a lecturer at the S. G. Stroganov Moscow Institute of Applied Art. In 1912 he became an academician of the St. Petersburg Academy of Arts, and in 1916 a full member of the Academy of Arts. From 1923 he lived in Riga. Although he belonged to the Society for Travelling Art Exhibitions (Peredvizhniki) from 1880–89, his work (especially from the 1910s onward) is nevertheless characterised rather by the impressionist manner of the Union of Russian Artists (VKL). He was also a founding member of this society (1903). At the same time, he has also been called one of the best-known members of the Peredvizhniki group. He painted genre scenes, portraits, interiors and landscapes, mostly in a bright and sunny plein-air style. The best part of his work was created during the period when he was active in the VKL.
