Eduard Wiiralt

1898–1954 · Estonian artist (1898-1954)

Eduard Wiiralt was born on 20 March 1898 in Saint Petersburg Governorate, the son of Sophie-Elisabeth Asur, a maid at the Robidets manor, and Anton Wiralt, a manor employee. In 1909 the family moved to live near Varangu manor in Järvamaa. E. Wiiralt studied at the Koeru Education Society school and at the Koeru German private school. In the autumn of 1915 he entered the Tallinn City School of Applied Art. In 1918 E. Wiiralt took part in the Estonian War of Independence. In 1919 he appeared for the first time in Tallinn with four graphic works at a survey exhibition of Estonian art held at the premises of the Girls' Commercial School. In the autumn of 1919 Wiiralt entered the newly opened Pallas art school in Tartu, from where, in the spring of 1922, he went to the Dresden Academy of Fine Arts for further training. In the autumn of 1923 the artist returned to his homeland and, in the spring of the following year, graduated from the Pallas school. In the autumn of 1924 Wiiralt began working as head of the Pallas graphic art studio, while also being active as a book graphic artist. In the spring of 1925 Wiiralt received a scholarship from the Board of the Fine Arts Foundation for a trip to Paris, where he arrived in the autumn of that year and stayed to live. In the autumn of 1936, while the artist was abroad, his first solo exhibition in Estonia took place at the Tallinn Art Hall, organised by Alfred Rõude, with more than 300 works exhibited. In the summer of 1938 Wiiralt travelled from France to Morocco. In the autumn of 1939, before World War II, the artist returned to Estonia via Paris, where he stayed until 1944. Having briefly stayed in Vienna and Stockholm and travelled in Lapland, Wiiralt spent the last decade of his life again working in Paris, where he died on 8 January 1954 and was buried in the Père-Lachaise Cemetery in Paris.
