August Vomm

1906–1976 · Estonian sculptor (1906-1976)
Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum; Vernissage; Virumaa Muuseumid

trained at Pallas

Before World War II, August Vomm's oeuvre consisted mainly of portrait busts and about a dozen labour-intensive gravestones. He depicted Ruts Bauman, Ants Lauter, Hugo Laur, Gustav Ernesaks, Romulus Tiitus, Ferdi Sannamees and others. As one of the few artists who spent the war years in his homeland, August Vomm adapted to the new demands right in the first post-war years. In 1945 he joined a brigade of sculptors, creating for the Estonia Theatre reliefs symbolising the union republics. In 1947 a monument to those fallen in the Great Patriotic War was completed in Viljandi, in 1952 a monument to Lenin, again created by the brigade method, was unveiled in Tartu, as well as, together with Lagle Israel, a memorial to Nikolai Burdenko on Toome Hill in Tartu. The monuments to the revolutionary figures Johannes Vares-Barbarus and Hans Pöögelmann in Tallinn, created by him, have since been relocated. For the Lenin jubilee exhibition in 1970, Vomm created a giant oak portrait bust of V. I. Lenin, for which, together with Elmar Kits and Avo Keerend, he was awarded the State Prize and the honorary title of Merited Artist of the Estonian SSR, as well as all-Union recognition in the press.
