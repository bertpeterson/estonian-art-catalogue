Gori

1894–1944
Allee galerii; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Haus Galerii; Pärnu Muuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum

Grigori (Georg) Tõnisson was born in Pärnu as the son of a grammar school caretaker. In 1911 his landscape vignettes and stories were printed in the Postimees supplement Sädemed, and soon his caricatures appeared in the newspapers Kilk and Meie Tallinna Ajaleht. In 1913 he moved to Tallinn and began working for the magazine Leek. In 1915 he was mobilised into the Tsarist army, and in 1915 he was taken prisoner of war by the Germans. In 1918 he returned to Tallinn and, together with Hendrik Saar (Kivilombi Ints), founded the satirical art magazine "Sipelgas" (15 issues were published), for which Gori was both a prose and picture author (in woodcut). From 1920 he was a member of the editorial board of the newspaper Vaba Maa, and in 1938 a member of the editorial board of Rahvaleht. In 1941 Gori was arrested; after his release he worked at the newspapers Rahva Hääl and Sirp ja Vasar. During the German occupation he was imprisoned twice; the artist's date of death is 7.10.1944, the cause of which (suicide?) remains unresolved. He is buried at the Tallinn Siselinna cemetery.
