Jaak Soans

1943– · Estonian sculptor (born 1943)
Dr. Fr. R. Kreutzwaldi Memoriaalmuuseum; Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum

trained at Estonian Academy of Arts

He became a renewer of Estonian modernist sculpture in the 1970s. Alongside formal searches based on the interaction of plastic volumes and space, an exceptional pursuit of elegance appeared in his work. Soans has managed to poeticise bronze as a sculptural material of value, and his bronze sculptures are created with a pure silhouette effect. He broadened the thematics and communicativeness of Estonian sculpture, bringing into his works existential reflections on the possibilities of man and the humanities in a technology-based, alienating world. Soans can be viewed as an intellectual and a teacher, followed both by his students and his colleagues. The public knows him predominantly as a monumentalist. He has created more than 30 monuments and public sculptures and immortalised great national figures: for example the monuments to A. H. Tammsaare (1978), Voldemar Panso (1980) and Michel Sittow (1982) in Tallinn, the monuments to Kristjan Jaak Peterson (1983) and Jakob Hurt (1994) in Tartu, the monument to August Gailit in Valga (2009). He has also created abstract large-scale forms in Estonia, Finland, the Netherlands and Argentina. Soans's work of recent years carries a cheerful intellectual play with the field of meaning of figures. Bronze of past decades has been replaced by steel, wood and other less durable materials, as well as ready-made objects. In his spatial installations one encounters recognisable recurring motifs, reflections on the nature of sculpture and its relations with time and space. Jaak Soans is professor emeritus of the Estonian Academy of Arts. He has been awarded the honorary title of Meritorious Artist of the ESSR (1980) and People's Artist of the ESSR (1986), the Kristjan Raud Art Prize (1974 and 1986), the grand prize of the Riga Sculpture Quadrennial.
