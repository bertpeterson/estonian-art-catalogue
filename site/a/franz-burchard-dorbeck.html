Franz Burchard Dörbeck

1799–1835 · Baltic German artist (1799-1835)
Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum

He was born in 1799 in Viljandi, the son of the tailor Friedrich Burchard Dörbeck and his mother Helena (Rakosnik), who was of Polish descent. He studied graphic art in St. Petersburg under Friedrich Neyer, and was an engraver of texts for state securities. Dörbeck's first marriage lasted from August to October 1819. In 1820–23 he lived in Riga, where he engraved visiting cards and portraits. In 1822 he made an aquatint of the Estonian K. J. Peterson. In 1823 he remarried in Riga and moved to Berlin, where he worked as a successful caricaturist (publisher Gropius). Lithograph series: Berliner Witze, Berliner Redensarten (1830), Album Academicum (1832), etc. In Berlin Dörbeck engraved portraits of Luther, Calvin and others. In 1823 his daughter Olga Helene was born, and in 1827 his son Roman Anastasius. In 1830 his second wife Jakobine died. In 1831 the artist married for the third time. In 1832 his son Franz was born. Suffering from tuberculosis, Dörbeck returned with Olga and Roman to Viljandi in 1835. He died in Viljandi. A memorial stone was unveiled to him at the Riia maantee cemetery.
