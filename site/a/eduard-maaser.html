Eduard Maaser

1916–1990 · Estonian artist (1916-1990)

trained at Pallas · trained at Tartu Art School

Herman Aunapuu was born on 12 March 1915 in Kokand, Turkestan, Central Asia, where his father was a stationmaster. The family moved to Tartu in 1921. In 1933 Aunapuu entered the Pallas Higher Art School, where he received a first-class education under Jaan Vahtra, Voldemar Mellik and Villem Ormisson. His main supervisor, however, was Ado Vabbe, under whom he also continued his studies at the Tartu State Art Institute from autumn 1944. In March 1945 Aunapuu was arrested and, by decision of a special tribunal, sentenced from 8 September to eight years in a corrective labour camp for betraying the homeland. He remained in labour camps in the Komi ASSR and Arkhangelsk Oblast until 1953. Herman Aunapuu first exhibited in 1940 at an exhibition organised by the Pallas Art Society, where donations and deposits were displayed — ranging from Konrad Mägi to Elmar Kits — which were meant to become the founding stock of the Tartu Art Museum then being established. Aunapuu was at that time still a student, yet he was already being compared to Endel Kõks and Elmar Kits. After the labour camps Aunapuu rarely exhibited; in fact he was pushed aside from exhibitions. He was never admitted to the Artists' Union. From 1971 onward the artist lived in seclusion near Tartu, in Peedu. Herman Aunapuu died on 29 December 1998 in Tartu and is buried at Raadi Maarja Cemetery.
