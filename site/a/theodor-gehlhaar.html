Theodor Gehlhaar

1805–1871 · Baltic German printmaker (1805-1871)
Eesti Ajaloomuuseum; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Eesti Rahva Muuseum; Narva Muuseum; Tartu Kunstimuuseum; Tartu Linnamuuseum

A Baltic German printmaker, painter and pharmacist. He was born as the son of a pharmacist of Swiss origin who was also a town councillor of Limbaži; his mother was the daughter of a local pastor. In 1828 Gehlhaar passed the examination for pharmacist's assistant at the University of Tartu, and in 1828, as an external candidate, the examination for pharmacist. In 1834 he became the owner of his father's pharmacy in Limbaži. He presumably studied at the drawing school of the University of Tartu under C. A. Senff, but as an artist he was mainly self-taught. In 1828 he published in Tallinn the first series of view prints in aquatint technique, published by Georg Eggers. The series included three views drawn by T. Gehlhaar himself, six sheets reproduced gouache paintings by Johannes Hau, and three engravings rendered sepia drawings or paintings by Karl Ferdinand von Kügelgen. In 1832/33 there appeared, compiled/executed by T. Gehlhaar and marketed by G. Eggers but published anonymously, a series of 12 sheets in the so-called ink-manner aquatint technique. In 1833–35 Gehlhaar also made individual sheets with views of Tallinn and its surroundings, as well as, in 1835, a series of views of Riga in aquatint technique. Around 1840 Georg Friedrich Schlater lithographed in colour in Tartu T. Gehlhaar's 12-sheet ethnographic series "Characteristic Scenes from the Life of the Peasants of Estonia and Livonia". Around 1850 T. Gehlhaar executed a series of 10 lithographs of views of Tallinn (publisher F. Kluge) and other individual sheets (lithographer F. J. Tempeltey). T. Gehlhaar has also created oil and gouache paintings. (machine translation of the holder's Estonian text, unedited)
