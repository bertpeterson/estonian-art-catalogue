Kristjan Teder

1901–1960 · Estonian painter (1901-1960)
Allee galerii; Eesti Kunsti Oksjonid; Eesti Kunstimuuseum; Haus Galerii; Tartu Kunstimuuseum; Vaal galerii

Kristjan TEDER (1901, Kuigatsi parish, Tartumaa – 1960, Tartu) was an Estonian painter and gardener who practised mainly flower painting, and to a lesser extent landscape, portrait and genre painting. His early work is New Objectivist, characterised by clearly defined forms and a dull brownish colour scheme. In the 1930s his manner of painting became impressionistically nuanced, the colouring bright and, in some works, vividly coloured, and the depictive style at times shows a naivist roughness. In the flower paintings made after World War II, Teder focused attention on bringing out spatiality and modelling with light and shadow. Alongside baroquely lush flower paintings, he also produced landscapes and townscapes in a more restrained colour range. In the second half of the 1950s Teder painted mainly vividly coloured flower pictures and landscapes. Teder received his art education at the Pallas Art School, first under Konrad Mägi and then under Ado Vabbe, and in Paris at Vassili Shukhaev's private academy. /text by Liisi Aibel, 2013/.
