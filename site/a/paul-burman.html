Paul and Karl Burman are an interesting example in our cultural history of how representatives of the then-declining Baltic German culture adapted to and blended into a rapidly developing national art scene. He began his career as an artist in Estonia at a time when Baltic German artistic life was fading and losing significance. Paul, in his cultural convictions and use of language, firmly belonged to the Baltic German circle. His studies and childhood home also connected him with Russian culture. The artist's family had Baltic German roots and close ties with Russia. Paul Burman never learned the Estonian language until his death, although during his lifetime he associated with many Estonian artists. His work, however, developed under the influence of inner logic and impulses and had no closer connection with the general trends of Estonian art.
